Pattern Blueprint

ADRE Grade 3 Exam Pattern & Marking Scheme

Before starting your practice sets, keep the standard SLRC Grade 3 test pattern in mind:

Total Questions 100 MCQs
Total Marks 100 / 150 Post specific
Duration 120 Min 2 Hours Total
Negative Marking -0.25 Per Wrong MCQ

Core Subjects Covered in Grade 3 Written Test:

  • 1
    General Knowledge & Current Affairs: Special focus on Assam History (Ahom dynasty, Moamoria rebellion, Treaty of Yandaboo), Geography of Brahmaputra and Barak basins, Assam National Parks, Census data, and State Government initiatives.
  • 2
    General English: Vocabulary, Idioms & Phrases, Prepositions, Spotting Errors, Active/Passive Voice, and Sentence Rearrangement.
  • 3
    Quantitative Aptitude / Mathematics (Class 10 Level): Percentages, Ratio and Proportion, Time and Work, Simple & Compound Interest, Profit & Loss, and Elementary Mensuration.
  • 4
    Logical Reasoning & Mental Ability: Number & Alphabet Series, Coding-Decoding, Blood Relations, Direction Sense, and Venn Diagrams.
  • 5
    Reading Comprehension & Social Studies: Class 10 Social Science curriculum, Indian Constitution, Heritage, and Reading Passages.

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQs)

Q1. Where can I find ADRE Grade 3 previous year papers with answer keys?

You can download the verified PDFs directly from the table above or attempt them interactively with automated scoring and negative marking on the PYQScore app.

Q2. Is there negative marking in the ADRE Grade 3 written exam?

Yes. There is a penalty of -0.25 marks for every wrong answer. Unattempted questions do not carry any penalty.

Q3. Can I take ADRE Grade 3 mock tests in Assamese?

Yes. On the PYQScore platform and app, all questions support an instant Assamese ↔ English toggle so candidates can check translations instantly during practice.

Q4. How does solving PYQs help in qualifying the ADRE cut-off?

Analyzing past papers helps candidates pinpoint high-yield recurring topics such as Assam history, state geography, and Class 10 arithmetic. It eliminates unexpected surprises on exam day and trains candidates to manage the 120-minute timer without making careless negative marking blunders.
